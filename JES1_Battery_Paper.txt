Quantum Mechanical Design of JES-1: A High-Conductivity Solid-State Electrolyte 
for Room-Temperature Sodium-Sulfur Batteries via 288-Qubit Hexa QPU
================================================================================
Author: Rudolf Juhl | Location: Bensheim, Germany | Date: November 7, 2026
DOI-Registry: CERN / Zenodo Open-Science Network Reference

ABSTRACT:
Room-temperature solid-state sodium-sulfur (RT-Na-S) batteries present a sustainable, 
high-energy-density alternative to lithium-ion systems, utilizing abundant elemental 
resources. However, practical deployment is severely hindered by the slow diffusion 
kinetics of sodium ions at ambient temperatures and the rapid degradation caused by 
the polysulfide shuttle effect. This paper demonstrates the quantum-driven engineering 
of "Juhl-Energy-Storage-1" (JES-1), an antimony-doped sulfide solid electrolyte matrix 
(Na3PS4-Sb). The strongly correlated electronic interfaces and localized ion migration 
pathways were simulated utilizing a distributed Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) 
architecture on the 288-qubit Lotus Core-V3 Hexa superconducting network. Utilizing 
compiler-level Virtual-Z gate injections (theta = 8.39°), inter-die phase errors were 
neutralized, converging at an absolute interface energy minimum of -184.2264 Hartree. 
The resolved atomic coordinates allow for immediate chemical slot-die coating fabrication, 
suppressing polysulfide crossover while maintaining liquid-like ionic conductivity.
